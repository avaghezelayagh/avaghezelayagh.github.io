Research

I combine genomic data with morphological and environmental datasets, and with methods from machine learning, to study how aquatic biodiversity evolved and how to conserve it.

01

Macroevolution of fishes

Phylogenomic trees of spiny-rayed fishes built from thousands of ultraconserved elements. I use them to test how mass extinctions, key innovations and new habitats shaped vertebrate diversity.

02

Species & conservation

Integrative species delimitation that combines genomes and morphology to decide which lineages are distinct. These decisions matter directly for endangered-species policy.

03

Machine learning for the fossil record

With David Jablonski, I am building an evolutionary framework for living and extinct bivalves (clams, mussels, oysters and scallops) that integrates fossils and modern species.
