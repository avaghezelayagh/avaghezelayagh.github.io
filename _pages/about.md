I am an AI + Science Postdoctoral Scholar at the [Data Science Institute](https://datascience.uchicago.edu/) and the [Department of the Geophysical Sciences](https://geosci.uchicago.edu/) at the University of Chicago, where I work with David Jablonski. My research applies genomic data to explore historical patterns relevant to evolutionary theory and to the conservation of aquatic biodiversity, especially in the face of today's heightened extinction risk.

At UChicago I use machine learning to build a comprehensive evolutionary framework for living and extinct bivalves: mussels, oysters, scallops and their relatives. I completed my BS and PhD in Ecology & Evolutionary Biology at Yale University in the lab of Thomas Near. There I specialized in the systematics, phylogenomics and macroevolution of fishes, from the radiation of spiny-rayed fishes after the end-Cretaceous extinction to species limits in North American darters.
